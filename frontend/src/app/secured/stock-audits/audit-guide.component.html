1. The process at a glance

Ten steps, from the plan to the register. The blue boxes are done at the shelf; the green boxes at the desk. Each step is explained below.

2. What auditing is for

The books say what should be on the shelf. A count says what is. The difference is the first sign of a billing mistake, a purchase not entered, damage, expiry or theft. Regular, small, written checks find these while they are still small and easy to trace. They also show the owner, an accountant or an inspector that the store looks after its stock.

A good audit has four parts: a plan (what, when, who), a count (on a sheet, by a person), a decision on every difference (who approved, and why), and a record (a signed certificate, filed).

5. Before you count

  • Two people if you can: one counts, one writes. A single person counting alone is the weakest case.
  • Tidy the shelf first: put returned and mixed batches where they belong. Take expired and damaged stock off the shelf and keep it in one marked box. Count it separately.
  • Billing carries on. The system fixes the book quantity at the moment you start the count. Sales and other changes made while you count are taken into account (see section 8), so they are not mistaken for shortages. Do not stop billing.
  • Start the count in the system first. The count sheet is printed from the quantities fixed at the start, so the paper and the system always agree.
  • Use the right store. Choose the audit's store at the top of the screen before you start; a count is done at one location.

6. Counting, step by step

  1. Open Stock audits. Find the audit (each has a number such as SA-0012) and press Do the audit.
  2. On the count screen press Start Count. The list of batches and their book quantities appears.
  3. Press Print the count sheet. It lists every batch with the book quantity, a box for your count and a box for remarks.
  4. Count each batch on the shelf. Count the packs and loose strips in the unit the sheet shows (the sheet says the pack size). Write the number in the Counted box. Write damaged, near-expiry or “not found” in Remarks.
  5. If a batch is not on the shelf, count it as 0. If you find a batch that is not on the sheet, write it at the bottom and tell the Store Head after the count: it means a purchase was not entered.
  6. Sign the sheet (Counted by, date and time) and give it to the Store Head.
  7. Enter the counts on the count screen. Leave a line as it is when the count matched. Check your entries against the paper before you submit. If you counted earlier than you are typing, set Counting finished at to the time you finished (never later than now); leave it blank if you are entering the counts right away.
  8. Press submit. Every line with a difference becomes an adjustment waiting for approval (see the next section). The audit is marked done.
  9. Open Variance on the audit row, read it with the Store Head, and handle each difference.
  10. When every adjustment is settled, the Store Head presses Sign off, prints the Certificate, and both people sign it.

Handling a difference properly

  • Look for the reason before you approve. Check recent purchases not entered, a return not entered, a bill made for the wrong batch, stock moved to another store, sales during the count (the Sold column).
  • Give it a real reason: Damaged, Expired, Missing, Returned. “Other” is for the rare case that fits none, and a note must say why. Too many “Other” write-offs raise an alert.
  • Never approve just to make the numbers clean. An approved adjustment changes the stock and cannot be quietly undone; it stays on the record with your name.
  • Reject an adjustment when the cause turns out to be a mistake in the count or an entry that should be corrected another way (enter the missing purchase instead).
  • Repeated shortages in the same product, shelf or shift are a pattern. Tell the Business Head and look at the weekly review.

8. Sales and changes while you count

A pharmacy cannot stop selling for a count, so the shelf changes while you are counting it. The system allows for this. A count is not measured against the book at the start but against what should be on the shelf when counting finished:

Expected = book at the start − sold since the start ± other movements since the start
What happens during the countHow it is handled
A saleTaken off the expected quantity (the Sold column on the variance report).
A customer return, a transfer to or from another store, or an approved adjustment such as an expiry write-offAdded to or taken off the expected quantity (the Other column).
A delivery entered during the countThe new batch is not on the sheet, so it is not counted. It is listed on the variance report under Received during the count for the next audit.
Hand-written bills (a power cut) not yet enteredThe system cannot know about them. Enter them before you settle the audit, or the shortage disappears by itself once they are keyed in.

Rules that make it work

  • Count and enter in the same session. The system does not know when you stood at each shelf, only the time you give as Counting finished at. If you counted at 10 and typed at 3, set it to 10.
  • Do not approve other adjustments for a batch while it is being counted. A batch in an open count shows Counting now in Adjustments, and asks you to press Approve a second time. It is allowed, and taken into account, but check first.
  • Hold purchase entry for the category being counted until the count is submitted. Write any delivery that arrives on the sheet.
  • Count quickly. The shorter the count, the smaller the corrections. Fast movers before opening; a full count after closing.
  • The limit: if you counted a line before a sale but typed it in after, the correction goes one way too far. This is why the finish time and the same-session rule matter.

9. Each audit in detail

Fast movers (weekly)

The list is the 30 products with the highest sales value in the last 90 days at this store. Count all batches of these products. It takes about 15 to 20 minutes. Differences here are the most important: these products move the most money.

Category rotation (weekly)

The system picks the next category in turn. Count every batch of every product in that category. When a category is finished the next audit moves to the next category, so the whole shop is covered over the cycle.

Scheduled drugs H, H1 and X (monthly)

  1. Run the audit as usual. The sheet lists only scheduled products and marks the schedule beside the name.
  2. Before the count, find the closing balance of each Schedule H1 and X item in the register. After the count, check: register opening + received − dispensed = register closing, and that the closing equals the shelf count.
  3. Any difference between the shelf, the system and the register goes to the pharmacist-in-charge and the Business Head on the same day. Do not adjust until it has been counted again.
  4. Keep the register, the sheet and the certificate together.

This guide describes the store's own routine. The rules that apply to your licence and registers are set by the drug authorities; ask your pharmacist-in-charge or accountant if you are unsure.

Expiry sweep (monthly)

  1. Run the audit: it lists every batch that has expired or will within 90 days.
  2. Take expired batches off the shelf into the marked box. Check near-expiry batches and mark them for quick sale or return.
  3. Return what the vendor takes back (record the return with the vendor). Write off the rest with the reason Expired.
  4. The Expired stock alert shows the value still sitting on the shelf; the goal is zero.

Full count (yearly, 31 March)

Everything is counted, with the owner present, over one day or a closed morning if possible. Split the shop into sections, give each section to two people, and keep the sheets in order. Large differences are expected on the first full count; the Business Head settles them all. Print the stock valuation as at 31 March from Stock audits › Register for the accountant.

  • Numbered: every audit has a number, SA-0001, SA-0002 and so on, that runs on for the whole business. The same number is on all three documents.
  • The certificate is kept exactly as printed. A reprint is the same file, so an old copy can always be matched to the original. If something was wrong, do not edit; raise an adjustment and write a note.
  • File them together in this order: certificate on top, variance report, count sheet, scheduled-drug register page. Keep them in a folder for each financial year.
  • How long: keep the paper for at least six years. Your accountant will tell you if your books need longer.
  • The register (Stock audits › Register) lists every audit of a financial year with its number, result and certificate, and downloads to Excel for the accountant or an inspector.

12. When something goes wrong

The sheet is missing a batch I can see on the shelf
A purchase was not entered. Enter the purchase invoice, then it appears in the next count. Write it on the sheet and tell the Store Head.
A product shows negative stock
More was sold or written off than was bought: usually a purchase not entered or a bill against the wrong batch. Find the purchase, enter it, then count the product.
I started a count by mistake or could not finish it
Leave it. A count that is not submitted changes nothing. Start again later; if the audit has to be dropped, the Business Head can skip it with a reason.
The Sign off button is missing
Either some adjustments are still waiting for approval (the row says how many), or the audit has not been submitted.
It says a different person must sign
The person who counted cannot also verify. Ask the Store Head or Business Head to sign off.
A big shortage in scheduled drugs
Count again with a second person, check the register, and tell the pharmacist-in-charge and Business Head the same day before approving anything.
I found a mistake after the certificate was signed
Do not change the certificate. Raise a new adjustment with the reason and a note, and file it with the certificate.
The shop was busy and counts changed while I counted
The variance report already allows for it: look at the Sold and Other columns, and the Expected quantity. If you counted before you typed the counts in, set Counting finished at to when you counted. See section 8.

13. Year-end checklist (31 March)

  1. Every planned audit of the year is done or skipped with a reason (Stock audits › Register).
  2. No audit is left “Not certified”.
  3. The full count is done and all its large differences are settled.
  4. Expired stock is off the shelf and written off or returned.
  5. The stock valuation as at 31 March is downloaded from the Register and given to the accountant.
  6. The scheduled-drug registers are balanced and filed.
  7. The certificates, variance reports and count sheets are in the year’s folder.
  8. The Weekly review is signed for every week of the year.
  9. No hand-written bills are waiting to be entered.

14. Words used

Book quantity
What the system says should be on the shelf for a batch: bought, less sold, plus approved adjustments.
Count sheet
The printed list you count on, with the book quantity fixed at the moment the count started.
Expected quantity
What should be on the shelf when counting finished: the book at the start, less sold, plus or minus other movements since.
Variance
The difference between the count and the expected quantity. Short means less on the shelf; excess means more.
Large difference
A product out by more than ₹500 at cost (or any difference in a scheduled-drug audit). It needs a second count and the Business Head.
Recount
The second count of a large difference, by another person. It replaces the first.
Certificate
The one-page record, signed by the person who counted and the person who verified, kept exactly as printed.
Audit number
SA-0012: the number of the audit within the business, on every document.
At cost
Valued at what the store paid for it (purchase cost per unit), not at the selling price.
